The letter gets written after the patient has gone

Every ward round, clinic and discharge ends with the same job: turning what happened into a letter for the GP, a specialist or the patient. The facts are usually already written down, scribbled in shorthand between patients. Shaping them into a properly structured letter still takes time out of a day that's already full.

Clinidraft is a prototype that tries to close that gap. Give it the shorthand and it writes a first draft in the right format, so the doctor's time goes on checking and correcting rather than starting from a blank page.

How it works

  1. 1

    Jot the findings

    Type the notes the way a doctor already writes them: short lines, abbreviations and all.

  2. 2

    Clinidraft writes the draft

    It shapes the notes into the right layout for the letter type, and marks anything the notes didn't cover instead of guessing.

  3. 3

    A doctor checks and signs

    Nothing leaves the page on its own. Every draft is reviewed, corrected and approved by a doctor. Clinidraft drafts; the doctor decides.

What it is, and what it isn't

Paperwork is a sensible place for AI to help, but only inside clear limits. These are Clinidraft's.

  • A drafting aid, not a decision-maker

    It never diagnoses, prescribes, sends or files anything. A qualified clinician must review and approve every draft before it is used.

  • No real patient data

    It isn't connected to any clinical system. Only ever give it made-up or fully anonymised details.

  • It flags what it doesn't know

    The AI modes are told to use only the notes they're given and to write [not documented] instead of inventing anything. Small models still make mistakes, so every line needs checking.

  • Private by design

    The site has no server and stores nothing. On-device AI keeps your notes inside your browser. Claude mode sends them only to Anthropic, using your own key.
